# The figure suite, and the conventions it follows

Every figure in the paper is produced by a script in `scripts/figures/`, writes
the numbers it drew to a sibling `.json`, and can be regenerated by anyone with
the repository. Two of the seven need a GPU measurement first; the rest run on
a laptop in seconds.

```bash
python scripts/figures/make_all.py            # everything that has data
python scripts/figures/make_all.py --only leverage quintic
```

`make_all.py` prints which claims currently have a figure behind them and which
are waiting on a measurement, so the gap between what the paper says and what
it can show is one command away rather than a thing to remember.

## What each figure carries

| figure | claim | needs |
|---|---|---|
| `fig1_leverage` | the row norms of a Muon update **are** leverage scores; row normalisation is inert on wide matrices; a fused QKV projection starves Q and K | nothing — CPU, seconds |
| `fig2_quintic` | Muon's iteration cannot reach the polar factor at any budget, and the resulting step-size drift is 23% over condition 1–1000 | nothing — CPU, seconds |
| `fig3_curvature` | the advantage is a **direction** effect (lower NDS), not a step-size effect | `measure_curvature.py` on a GPU |
| `fig4_results` | the 124M comparison, drawn with its noise floor and its untuned row visible | nothing — reads `artifacts/measured.json` |
| `fig5_inversion` | cautious masking's sign inverts between 1.17M and 124M | nothing |
| `fig6_horizon` | whether the margin survives longer training — including a panel that says *not measured* where it is not | optional `astro_lab_state.json` |
| `fig7_drift` | the non-convergence observed during real training rather than on planted matrices | `measure_drift.py` on a GPU |

Figures 1 and 2 are the ones to point a skeptical reader at first. They are
verifications of identities rather than experiments, so they do not inherit any
of the scale, seed-count or tuning caveats that the training results do.

## The two GPU measurements

```bash
# Figure 3 -- the curvature decomposition. ~40 min at 124M on a T4.
python scripts/measure_curvature.py --optimizers adamw muon normuon astro \
    --steps 600 --probe-every 50 --out curvature.json

# Figure 7 -- update-norm drift during training. ~15 min.
python scripts/measure_drift.py --optimizers muon astro --steps 300 \
    --every 10 --out drift.json
```

Copy the JSON next to the repository and re-run `make_all.py`.

Both plotters are exercised in `tests/test_figures.py` against synthetic data
of the right shape, so a broken plotter is caught before anyone spends an hour
of a free-tier session on the run that feeds it.

## Conventions

**One colour per optimizer, everywhere.** Adam blue and Muon orange follow the
convention of the curvature literature, so a reader arriving from those papers
does not need our key. Defined once in `scripts/figures/style.py`.

**Compare at matched validation loss, not matched step.** A better optimizer is
at a lower loss by step *t*, where the landscape is genuinely different, so a
step-matched comparison of any local quantity is confounded.
`style.align_on_loss` interpolates both trajectories onto the shared loss range
and **raises** rather than extrapolating when they do not overlap.

**Numbers on the figure.** Every quantitative claim in a caption should be
readable off the panel without the caption: bars carry their values, ratio
panels carry their average, and trends carry their endpoints.

**Draw the uncertainty you actually have.** Figure 4 shows all three seeds as
points with the runs joined, not a mean with an error bar that three samples
cannot support, and it shades the measured ±0.0021 cross-session noise floor.
Figure 6 leaves a panel blank with the command that would fill it.

**Say when something is not measured.** A missing measurement prints `SKIP` with
the command that produces it, and `fig6_horizon` draws "not yet measured" into
the panel rather than quietly plotting two points where three belong.

## Writing register

The reference for prose and structure is Wang et al., *Why Muon Outperforms
Adam: A Curvature Perspective* (arXiv 2606.04662). What we take from it:

- **Each empirical subsection ends in a boxed one-sentence Observation.** Not a
  summary of the section — the single claim the section licenses.
- **`Experiment setup.` / `Experiment findings.`** as bold run-in headers, so
  the method and the result never blur together.
- **Decomposition as narrative.** Take the headline quantity, split it into two
  interpretable terms, show which term carries the gap, then split *that* term
  again. The paper's spine is a sequence of decompositions, not a list of wins.
- **Verify assumptions in-text before using them.** Theory sections state an
  assumption and immediately show the figure that supports it on real
  checkpoints, rather than deferring to an appendix.
- **Establish that the model describes reality before interpreting its terms.**
  Their Figure 1(a) checks predicted against realized loss decrease *first*;
  only then do 1(b) and 1(c) attribute the difference. Our `fig3_curvature`
  panel (a) does the same job and must stay first for the same reason.
