\textbf{Abstract.}
Matrix-structured optimizers such as \Muon, \NorMuon, and \AdaMuon transform
matrix-valued updates rather than treating every parameter independently. We
document the development of \ASTRO, a Muon-family optimizer, together with an
experimental framework for testing whether an apparent optimizer improvement
survives shared hyperparameters, longer horizons, independent seeds, model
scale, and wall-clock accounting.

The current \ASTROv recipe keeps Muon-style spectral updates, splits fused
Q/K/V blocks before the polar transform, applies a norm-preserving row-wise
second moment after that transform, and routes non-operator parameters through
an Adam-like scalar path. At GPT-2 124M and 900 steps, the completed five-setting
shared grid gives a mean paired validation-loss difference of
\good{-0.0816} versus Muon, with ASTRO-v2 better in \good{3/5} settings. The
best ASTRO-v2 setting in that grid reaches \good{5.8102} validation loss versus
Muon's best \good{5.9286}, although those two minima occur at different
hyperparameters and are therefore not a paired comparison. A separate targeted
three-setting mechanism study gives mean paired deltas of \good{-0.2208} for
ASTRO-v2, -0.2137 for ASTRO-MB, and -0.2047 with the post-polar variance power
removed. The same study measures about \textbf{6.4\%} additional wall-clock per
900-step ASTRO-v2 run.

The evidence is promising but not yet a universal optimizer claim. The earlier
ASTRO recipe loses to Muon in a tuned 300-step benchmark; the 2700-step horizon
is unfinished; seven frozen-config Muon seeds are measured while matched
ASTRO-v2 seeds remain pending; and a clean 355M comparison must be rerun under a
matching code fingerprint. The emerging hypothesis is therefore narrower:
ASTRO-v2 may improve \emph{robustness to hyperparameter choice}, while a simpler
subset of its recipe carries most of the gain.

\section{Introduction}

Muon made a simple idea practical: hidden weight matrices can be optimized as
matrices rather than only as collections of scalar coordinates
\citep{jordan2024muon}. Subsequent work has improved Muon's scaling and
post-orthogonalization adaptivity, including large-scale Muon training
\citep{liu2025scalable}, \NorMuon \citep{li2025normuon}, and \AdaMuon
\citep{si2025adamuon}. At the same time, comparative studies show that optimizer
rankings depend on search budget, model scale, data budget, and training horizon
\citep{wen2025fantastic}.

That makes optimizer research unusually easy to overstate. A method can look
better because its learning-rate range is better centered, because an auxiliary
Adam-like path was tuned differently, because the comparison ends at a favorable
intermediate point, or simply because the method performs more work per step.
ASTRO began as an attempt to add additional control to Muon-family updates. The
project changed direction as experiments invalidated parts of the original
explanation.

This report therefore treats the \emph{research path} as part of the artifact.
Negative runs are retained, shared configurations are distinguished from shared
seeds, code fingerprints are recorded, missing measurements remain visibly
missing, and step-matched loss is separated from compute-matched efficiency.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A PyTorch implementation of ASTRO that subclasses
  \texttt{torch.optim.Optimizer} for state and parameter-group management while
  implementing the matrix update explicitly.
  \item A modern 124M comparison to Muon, NorMuon, and AdaMuon, including the
  path from earlier ASTRO variants to ASTRO-MB and ASTRO-v2.
  \item A completed 300/600/900-step shared-configuration horizon showing
  negative mean paired deltas at all three measured horizons, while preserving
  the fact that ASTRO-v2 does not win every setting.
  \item A mechanism study showing that ASTRO-MB captures most of the current
  ASTRO-v2 gain and that removing variance-power adaptation preserves most of it.
  \item A reproducible paper pipeline: committed JSON is the evidence layer,
  Matplotlib emits vector figures, W\&B is optional, and CI compiles the paper.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}

\subsection{Muon}
For a matrix-valued gradient $G_t$, Muon maintains momentum and applies a short
Newton--Schulz-style matrix iteration approximating the polar factor. If
$M=U\Sigma V^\top$, the idealized direction is $UV^\top$. Practical Muon uses
this path for hidden operators and an AdamW-like auxiliary path for embeddings,
biases, norms, and other parameters \citep{jordan2024muon,liu2025scalable}.

Large-scale Muon work shows that weight decay and update-RMS scaling matter for
scalability, and reports substantial compute-efficiency gains over AdamW in its
own scaling-law setting \citep{liu2025scalable}. Those results make Muon a
stronger baseline than a small-scale prototype implementation.

\subsection{NorMuon and AdaMuon}
\NorMuon adds neuron-wise adaptivity after orthogonalization while preserving the
total update norm \citep{li2025normuon}. \AdaMuon uses finer-grained adaptivity
on the orthogonalized direction \citep{si2025adamuon}. They are useful ASTRO
baselines because the present question is not merely whether adaptation exists,
but where it is placed and whether it widens the region of stable
hyperparameters.

\subsection{Why horizon, scale, and compute matter}
Optimizer rankings can change with training budget and scale
\citep{wen2025fantastic}. A more expensive optimizer can also win at equal steps
while losing at equal wall-clock. We therefore treat 124M/300, 124M/900,
124M/2700, and 355M/900 as distinct empirical questions and report measured run
time alongside loss.

\section{ASTRO-v2}

\subsection{Parameter routing}
The reference implementation is \texttt{class Astro(torch.optim.Optimizer)} in
\texttt{scripts/astro\_lab.py}. Hidden two-dimensional operators take the
spectral path. Embeddings, the tied output head, biases, normalization
parameters, and other non-operators take an Adam-like scalar path. GPT-2
\texttt{Conv1D} projections are interpreted in operator orientation, and fused
attention projections can be split into Q, K, and V blocks before the spectral
transform.

\subsection{Spectral path}
ASTRO maintains momentum
\begin{equation}
M_t = \beta_1 M_{t-1} + (1-\beta_1)G_t,
\end{equation}
forms a Nesterov-style lookahead, and applies the approximate polar transform to
obtain $Q_t$. A row-wise second moment is tracked on the orthogonalized
direction,
\begin{equation}
v_t = \beta_2 v_{t-1} + (1-\beta_2)\operatorname{rowmean}(Q_t^2),
\end{equation}
then used to redistribute update energy,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde Q_t = Q_t \oslash \widehat v_t^{\gamma/2}.
\end{equation}
The Frobenius norm is restored afterward,
\begin{equation}
D_t = \widetilde Q_t\frac{\norm{Q_t}_F}{\norm{\widetilde Q_t}_F},
\end{equation}
so this stage primarily changes \emph{where} update energy is placed rather than
simply enlarging the global step.

\subsection{V2 is a recipe, not a second optimizer class}
ASTRO-v2 instantiates the same \texttt{Astro} class with
\begin{verbatim}
"astro_v2": {
  "betas": (0.9, 0.95),
  "cautious_wd": False,
}
\end{verbatim}
The earlier recipe used $\beta_1=0.95$ on both spectral and scalar groups even
though Muon's Adam-like auxiliary path uses $0.9$. ASTRO-v2 removes that
asymmetry and disables cautious weight decay. \texttt{astro\_muon\_betas}
(ASTRO-MB) isolates the beta alignment, while \texttt{astro\_v2\_gamma0}
removes the variance-power effect.

\section{Experimental protocol}

\subsection{Model and data}
The principal experiments train GPT-2-style causal language models from scratch
on FineWeb-Edu with GPT-2 BPE. The main model is approximately 124M parameters,
with 12 layers, 12 heads, width 768, and sequence length 512. Validation is
pinned to the same first 400,000 tokens at every horizon so changing training
budget cannot silently change the evaluation text.

\subsection{Pairing and provenance}
We distinguish two experimental units:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{shared configurations}: optimizers see the same learning rate,
  weight decay, and scalar-path multiplier; this probes sensitivity and
  robustness;
  \item \textbf{shared seeds}: frozen optimizer-specific configurations are
  rerun under the same stochastic seeds; this probes reproducibility.
\end{itemize}
Three configurations are not three seeds.

The current 124M horizon and seed-replication evidence were produced by
\texttt{astro\_lab.py} fingerprint \texttt{7b321ff7fda3}. A partial 355M run used
fingerprint \texttt{56581ade7398}; because that provenance differs, the partial
355M numbers are excluded from paper-facing evidence and a clean rerun is
required.

\subsection{Metrics}
The primary metric is validation cross-entropy loss. We additionally record
wall-clock seconds per run, paired deltas versus Muon, the number of settings won,
and eventually time-to-target from frozen validation trajectories.

\section{Fused QKV allocation}
A fused attention projection stores Q, K, and V in one matrix. A spectral
transform can be mathematically valid for that fused matrix while distributing
update energy unevenly among the operators inside it.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Example Q/K/V update allocation. Splitting is applied before the
spectral transform.}
\label{tab:qkv}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Checkpoint & Fused Q/K/V & Split Q/K/V \\
\midrule
GPT-2 & .244/.226/\textbf{.530} & .318/.347/.336 \\
GPT-2 Medium & .245/.266/\textbf{.489} & .308/.369/.323 \\
Pythia-410M & .218/.248/\textbf{.534} & .356/.300/.344 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Gradient V/Q ratios in these examples are approximately $2.65\times$,
$3.27\times$, and $3.28\times$. Random-initialization controls also show V-skew,
so training is not claimed as the sole cause.

\obs{Fused spectral treatment can produce strongly imbalanced Q/K/V allocation,
while block-wise treatment restores substantially more balanced allocation. The
imbalance is architecture- and stage-dependent rather than purely learned during
training.}

\section{Research journey and corrections}

\paragraph{Shared learning rate was not enough.}
Early runs forced Muon and ASTRO to share one learning rate despite different
spectral and scalar-path calibration. Opposite conclusions appeared at different
fixed rates, motivating optimizer-specific tuning and shared-configuration
robustness analysis.

\paragraph{The scalar path mattered more than expected.}
ASTRO originally used $\beta_1=0.95$ for both matrix and scalar groups, whereas
Muon's auxiliary Adam path uses $0.9$. Aligning this behavior produced a larger
change than several more elaborate spectral modifications.

\paragraph{Cautious weight decay was not a free safety mechanism.}
A masked decay rule changes effective regularization unless survivors are
rescaled. ASTRO-v2 therefore disables cautious weight decay rather than treating
it as a harmless default.

\paragraph{Variance power is not yet the headline mechanism.}
Setting $\gamma=0$ loses only about 0.016 validation loss on average relative to
full ASTRO-v2 in the targeted run while preserving most of the advantage versus
Muon.

\paragraph{Provenance is part of the experiment.}
The repository records a source fingerprint at startup. The mismatched partial
355M run is kept in the research log but excluded from the paper-facing scale
figure. This is intentionally stricter than silently treating all runs as
interchangeable.

\section{Limitations and remaining experiments}
The strongest current evidence remains at 124M. The complete 900-step grid is
configuration-paired rather than seed-paired. The independent-seed experiment is
only half complete. The 2700-step horizon is unmeasured, and the clean 355M
rerun has not yet been completed. ASTRO-v2 is also measurably more expensive per
step than Muon.

Before a submission claim is frozen, the minimum remaining experiments are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item finish all five 124M/2700 shared settings;
  \item run ASTRO-v2 seeds 100--106 to pair with the existing seven Muon seeds;
  \item complete a same-fingerprint 355M/900 paired scale study;
  \item generate frozen validation trajectories for loss-vs-step and
  loss-vs-wall-clock comparisons.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
ASTRO-v2 is currently \textbf{promising, not proven}. The strongest new result
is a completed 124M/900 shared grid with a mean paired delta of -0.0816 versus
Muon and 3/5 settings favoring ASTRO-v2. A separate targeted study suggests that
most of the gain is already present in a simpler beta-aligned variant and that
the largest separation occurs in an aggressive learning-rate regime. Together,
those observations support a testable robustness hypothesis more strongly than a
claim of universal optimizer dominance.

The methodological commitment is simpler: the final paper will show the journey,
not only the winner. Negative results, protocol corrections, provenance
mismatches, compute overhead, and unfinished cells remain visible because an
optimizer comparison is most useful when it is allowed to fail.
