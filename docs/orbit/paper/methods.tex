\section{Method}

\subsection{Muon matrix update}
ORBIT retains Muon as the base update for matrix-valued parameters. For a matrix parameter
$W_t$ with gradient $G_t$ and momentum state $m_t$, the implementation forms
\begin{equation}
m_t=\mu m_{t-1}+G_t,\qquad
U_t=\alpha(W_t)\,\operatorname{NS}_5\!\left(G_t+\mu m_t\right),
\label{eq:muon-candidate}
\end{equation}
where $\mu=0.95$, $\operatorname{NS}_5$ denotes five iterations of the quintic
Newton--Schulz polar map, and
$\alpha(W)=\sqrt{\max(1,n_{\mathrm{row}}/n_{\mathrm{col}})}$ is the standard
aspect-ratio scaling used by the Muon implementation~\cite{jordan2024muon}. ORBIT changes
this candidate only for query and key projections. Other two-dimensional hidden operators
use the ordinary Muon update, while embeddings, the tied language-model head, biases, and
normalization vectors use the auxiliary AdamW path described in
Section~\ref{sec:training-setup}.

\subsection{RoPE-conditioned query--key geometry}
For a query at position $i$ attending to a causal key at position $j\le i$, define the
non-negative displacement $\Delta=i-j$. The implementation applies $R_f(i)$ to the query
and $R_f(j)$ to the key, so orthogonality of the RoPE rotations gives
\begin{equation}
  s_{ij,f}
  =(R_f(i)q_{i,f})^\top(R_f(j)k_{j,f})
  =q_{i,f}^{\top}R_f(j-i)k_{j,f}
  =q_{i,f}^{\top}R_f(-\Delta)k_{j,f}.
  \label{eq:rope-score}
\end{equation}
For an unrotated query perturbation $\delta q_f$,
$\delta s_f=\delta q_f^\top R_f(-\Delta)k_f$. Therefore the expected squared score
movement is a quadratic form whose metric depends on the opposite-side second moment. Let
$S_{Q,f}$ and $S_{K,f}$ denote exponential-moving-average $2\times2$ uncentered second-moment matrices of the
unrotated query and key coordinates. ORBIT defines
\begin{align}
M_{Q,f} &= \mathbb{E}_{\Delta}\!\left[
R_f(-\Delta)S_{K,f}R_f(-\Delta)^\top\right],\\
M_{K,f} &= \mathbb{E}_{\Delta}\!\left[
R_f(-\Delta)^\top S_{Q,f}R_f(-\Delta)\right].
\label{eq:orbit-metrics}
\end{align}
The expectation is approximated by a uniform average over the fixed log-spaced
causal-distance grid
$\mathcal{D}=\{1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128\}$. The eight nonzero offsets span short to
moderate causal separations on a logarithmic scale while keeping transport cost fixed;
$\Delta=0$ is omitted so that this average probes nonzero relative-position transport.
This is a design approximation rather than an empirical estimate of the token-pair distance
distribution: activation second moments are estimated over tokens, then transported across
the chosen distance grid.

The resulting $M_{Q,f}$ and $M_{K,f}$ are output-coordinate factors, not complete
parameter-space pullbacks. For example, $\delta q_f=\delta W_{Q,f}x$ means an exact
quadratic metric on $\delta W_{Q,f}$ would also contain statistics of the input activation
$x$. ORBIT deliberately keeps only the frequency-local $2\times2$ output-side factor and
applies it as a left preconditioner. This is the tractable local approximation evaluated in
the experiments. Second moments are
updated once per logical training step with EMA coefficient $\beta=0.95$ after the first
observation. In the 355M runs, gradient-checkpoint recomputation is excluded from statistics
collection so that checkpointing does not change the EMA cadence.

\subsection{ORBIT preconditioning}
Let $U_Q$ and $U_K$ be the Muon candidates from Equation~\ref{eq:muon-candidate}. For each
head and RoPE frequency pair,
\begin{align}
\widehat U_{Q,f} &= M_{Q,f}^{-1/2}U_{Q,f},\\
\widehat U_{K,f} &= M_{K,f}^{-1/2}U_{K,f}.
\end{align}
The metric is local to the pre-softmax rotary Q/K score; it is not a full-model Fisher
matrix, Hessian, or exact natural-gradient metric. The matrices are regularized by
$10^{-5}I$, their effective condition number is capped at 100, and the inverse square root
is evaluated analytically for each symmetric positive $2\times2$ matrix. For the
regularized effective metric $M$, the choice
$T=M^{-1/2}$ satisfies $T^\top M T=I$, so the local quadratic form is whitened rather
than inverted as aggressively as an $M^{-1}$ step. The ORBIT-specific structural constants
used throughout the campaign---$\beta=0.95$, functional power 1, $\epsilon=10^{-5}$,
condition cap 100, and $\mathcal D$ above---were fixed protocol values and were not selected
using held-out matched-confirmation outcomes.

A single joint restoration factor then preserves the total Q/K update budget:
\begin{equation}
\rho =
\sqrt{
\frac{\|U_Q\|_F^2+\|U_K\|_F^2}
     {\|\widehat U_Q\|_F^2+\|\widehat U_K\|_F^2}
},
\qquad
\widetilde U_Q=\rho\widehat U_Q,\quad
\widetilde U_K=\rho\widehat U_K.
\label{eq:joint-restore}
\end{equation}
Because $\|U\|_F=\|\operatorname{vec}(U)\|_2$, the Frobenius norm is the Euclidean
magnitude of the matrix update when viewed as a parameter vector. The functional metric
therefore changes the geometry of the joint Q/K update while the restoration fixes its
aggregate parameter-space step magnitude.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:orbit} gives the corresponding Q/K-pair update. Here
$\epsilon=10^{-5}$ and $\mathcal D=\{1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128\}$ in the reported experiments.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{ORBIT update for a RoPE query--key projection pair}
\label{alg:orbit}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Weights $W_Q,W_K$; gradients $G_Q,G_K$; unrotated activations $q,k$
\Require State $m_Q,m_K,S_Q,S_K$; hyperparameters $\eta,\lambda,\mu,\beta,\epsilon,\mathcal D$
\Ensure Updated $W_Q,W_K$ and state $m_Q,m_K,S_Q,S_K$
\State $S_Q,S_K \gets \operatorname{EMASecondMoments}(S_Q,S_K;q,k;\beta)$
\State $m_Q \gets \mu m_Q + G_Q$; \quad $m_K \gets \mu m_K + G_K$
\State $U_Q \gets \operatorname{MuonCandidate}(G_Q+\mu m_Q)$
\State $U_K \gets \operatorname{MuonCandidate}(G_K+\mu m_K)$
\For{each attention head $h$ and RoPE frequency pair $f$}
    \State $M_{Q,f} \gets |\mathcal D|^{-1}\!\sum_{\Delta\in\mathcal D}
    R_f(-\Delta)S_{K,f}R_f(-\Delta)^\top+\epsilon I$
    \State $M_{K,f} \gets |\mathcal D|^{-1}\!\sum_{\Delta\in\mathcal D}
    R_f(-\Delta)^\top S_{Q,f}R_f(-\Delta)+\epsilon I$
    \State $T_{Q,f} \gets \operatorname{InvSqrt}_{2\times2}(M_{Q,f})$
    \State $T_{K,f} \gets \operatorname{InvSqrt}_{2\times2}(M_{K,f})$
    \State $\widehat U_{Q,f} \gets T_{Q,f}U_{Q,f}$; \quad
    $\widehat U_{K,f} \gets T_{K,f}U_{K,f}$
\EndFor
\State $\rho \gets
\sqrt{\dfrac{\|U_Q\|_F^2+\|U_K\|_F^2}
{\|\widehat U_Q\|_F^2+\|\widehat U_K\|_F^2}}$
\State $\widetilde U_Q \gets \rho\widehat U_Q$; \quad
$\widetilde U_K \gets \rho\widehat U_K$
\State $W_Q \gets (1-\eta\lambda)W_Q-\eta\widetilde U_Q$
\State $W_K \gets (1-\eta\lambda)W_K-\eta\widetilde U_K$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Cost and ablation variants}
The additional ORBIT state consists of two $2\times2$ second-moment matrices for every
attention head and RoPE frequency pair. Its distinctive per-step matrix operations are
therefore local $2\times2$ transforms rather than dense model-width preconditioners.

Three controls isolate the update components. \textbf{Identity} keeps the ORBIT training
path and statistics collection but disables functional preconditioning. \textbf{No-RoPE}
uses Q/K second-moment metrics without relative-position rotation. \textbf{Diagonal} retains
RoPE-aware per-frequency scaling while removing off-diagonal coupling within each
$2\times2$ pair.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Models and data}
All reported comparisons use the same decoder implementation within a given size. The
124M model has 12 layers, 12 attention heads, width 768, and approximately 124M parameters.
The 355M model has 24 layers, 16 heads, width 1024, and uses gradient checkpointing. Both
use context length 512, GPT-2 token embeddings with tied input/output weights, pre-norm
Transformer blocks, GELU MLPs with $4d$ hidden width, and zero dropout. Learned absolute
position embeddings are replaced by RoPE with base 10,000. Attention uses separate
$q_{\mathrm{proj}}$, $k_{\mathrm{proj}}$, and $v_{\mathrm{proj}}$ matrices.

The corpus is \texttt{HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-edu}, configuration
\texttt{sample-10BT}, pinned to revision \texttt{v1.0.0}. Text is tokenized with the
GPT-2 tokenizer. A fixed stream of 12.4M tokens is shared across runs: the first 400,000
tokens form the validation pool and the next 12,000,000 tokens form the training pool.
Every training step samples random contiguous 512-token windows from that pool with a
seeded generator. In paired cells, methods with the same run seed therefore receive the
same sequence of sampled training windows. Evaluation uses a fixed, separate generator and
averages 20 validation batches, so all runs use the same evaluation-window sequence.

\subsection{Training setup}
\label{sec:training-setup}
All optimizers use the same warmup--cosine multiplier. The first 10\% of steps are warmup;
the remaining schedule follows a cosine decay ending at $0.1$ times the peak learning rate.
For ORBIT, the auxiliary AdamW rate is stored as a fixed ratio to the matrix learning rate,
so scheduling the matrix rate applies the identical multiplier to both paths. Global gradient
norm is clipped at 1.0. Training uses CUDA autocast in float16 with
gradient scaling. The 124M model uses batch size 8 (4,096 sampled tokens per optimizer
step); the 355M model uses batch size 4 (2,048 sampled tokens per step). The latter
difference is disclosed because it prevents interpreting the 355M result as a pure
model-size scaling experiment. Reported wall-clock time starts immediately before the
training loop and ends after the fixed 20-batch validation pass, so it is an end-to-end
per-run measure rather than optimizer-step time alone. Peak CUDA allocation is recorded
with \texttt{torch.cuda.max\_memory\_allocated} and reported in GiB. All twenty runs in
the primary matched-confirmation comparison were executed on NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs.

\subsection{Baselines and hyperparameter selection}
The broad campaign includes AdamW, Muon, NorMuon, AdaMuon, ASTRO, and ORBIT. ASTRO is a
previously developed Muon variant used only as a reference baseline; its matrix path combines
Nesterov-style momentum, a Newton--Schulz/polar transform, post-polar row-wise adaptation,
and norm-preserving redistribution, with an Adam-like auxiliary path for non-matrix
parameters.

Every optimizer receives the same number of tuned dimensions in the broad campaign.
Muon-family methods search learning rate in $[2\times10^{-3},10^{-1}]$, weight decay in
$[10^{-3},3\times10^{-1}]$, and auxiliary AdamW multiplier in $[0.02,1.5]$, with
log-uniform deterministic sampling. AdamW tunes learning rate, weight decay, and $\beta_2$.
AdaMuon uses its published natural learning-rate range but the same weight-decay and
auxiliary-rate policy. Five discovery trials are used per optimizer in the broad campaign.

The primary Muon--ORBIT comparison uses ten deterministic 900-step candidate
configurations drawn once from the Muon search space and applied identically to both update
rules on one fixed tuning seed. Muon and ORBIT independently attain their lowest tuning loss
at the same candidate, \MatchedSharedConfigID{}. That coincident winner is frozen before
the ten held-out matched-confirmation seeds are evaluated, and the same shared setting is
reused in the expanded ORBIT ablation. Thus the held-out comparison is an identical-recipe
test, while its uncertainty remains conditional on the preceding shared-grid selection.

\paragraph{Statistical analysis.}
The primary estimand is the paired per-run validation-loss difference, ORBIT minus Muon.
Reported statistics include the paired mean, sample standard deviation, paired-run win
count, and a two-sided 95\% Student-$t$ confidence interval for the mean paired difference.
The matched Muon--ORBIT comparison is the sole primary confirmatory contrast. Its confidence
interval is conditional on the frozen shared selected recipe and does not incorporate
uncertainty from recipe selection. Ablation intervals are secondary mechanism analyses and
are reported without multiplicity adjustment; the long-horizon and 355M cells are
descriptive transfer checks rather than high-powered inferential tests. End-to-end
wall-clock time and peak allocated CUDA memory are reported alongside loss.

All final comparisons use the same frozen implementation and a consistent run-record format.
Optimizer settings, random seeds, environment metadata, validation metrics, runtime,
memory use, and training trajectories are retained with each run. Results are merged only
after automated consistency checks verify the expected experiment structure, matched
configuration, finite metrics, and a common implementation/software state.
