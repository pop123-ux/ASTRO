\begin{abstract}
Matrix optimizers such as Muon exploit the spectral structure of parameter matrices while
treating query and key projections as ordinary matrix parameters. ORBIT introduces a
function-space extension of Muon for rotary attention: frequency-local $2\times2$ metrics
are constructed from query/key second moments transported through RoPE relative-position
rotations, applied to the candidate Q/K updates, and followed by joint Frobenius-norm
restoration. On RoPE-modified GPT-2-style decoders trained on FineWeb-Edu, the evaluation
separates optimizer mechanism from hyperparameter recipe. Muon is first tuned over a
ten-candidate 900-step grid; its selected configuration is then frozen before held-out
evaluation of either method. Under that shared recipe, the comparison reports \MatchedWins{}
ORBIT-favoring seeds and a paired ORBIT-minus-Muon mean difference of \MatchedDelta{} nats
(95\% CI [\MatchedCILow{}, \MatchedCIHigh{}]).
Mean end-to-end wall-clock difference is \MatchedRuntimeOverhead{}, with peak allocated
CUDA-memory difference \MatchedMemoryOverhead{}. Cross-configuration experiments quantify recipe
sensitivity, while the ablations isolate functional Q/K conditioning, RoPE-aware transport,
and full frequency-pair coupling. Longer-horizon and 355M experiments are reported as
secondary transfer checks. More broadly, the study tests \emph{function-aware optimization} as a design principle:
optimizer geometry is derived from the computation performed by a parameter, not only from
its tensor shape or gradient history.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Matrix optimizers have become a practical alternative to coordinate-wise adaptive methods
for Transformer training. Muon, for example, applies momentum followed by approximate
matrix orthogonalization to hidden two-dimensional parameters, and its scaled variants have
shown that spectral update geometry can remain effective at large language-model
scale~\cite{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon}. Yet this treatment is largely parameter-centric:
two matrices with the same shape receive the same class of update even when they participate
in qualitatively different computations.

Self-attention provides a particularly sharp example. Query and key projections jointly
determine pre-softmax attention scores, and RoPE rotates two-dimensional Q/K coordinate
pairs so that their interaction depends on relative position~\cite{su2021roformer}. For a
frequency pair $f$ and causal displacement $\Delta=i-j\ge0$, the implementation gives
$q_{i,f}^{\top}R_f(-\Delta)k_{j,f}$. Consequently, an equal-sized perturbation of $W_Q$ or
$W_K$ can induce very different functional movement depending on the opposite circuit's
statistics and on the RoPE rotation associated with token displacement. Standard Muon does
not model this interaction.

ORBIT incorporates that functional geometry on top of Muon's spectral update. It first
forms the ordinary Muon-style candidate Q/K directions, then preconditions each RoPE
frequency pair with a
compact metric derived from the uncentered second moment of the opposite circuit after
transport across a fixed log-spaced causal-distance grid. A single joint Frobenius restoration
prevents this conditioning from winning merely by increasing the total Q/K step norm. The distinctive ORBIT operations are local $2\times2$ matrix functions. This provides a concrete instance of \emph{function-aware optimization}:
the optimizer uses the known computation in which Q/K parameters participate to define a
local update geometry, rather than relying only on generic parameter shape or gradient
statistics.

Optimizer comparisons are unusually sensitive to tuning policy. For the primary comparison,
Muon and ORBIT are evaluated on the same predeclared ten-candidate grid on one tuning seed;
their independently selected winners coincide, yielding one shared recipe that is frozen
before held-out paired evaluation. Separate crossed-recipe experiments quantify
optimizer--recipe interaction. Full selection and evaluation details are given in
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-protocol}.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\paragraph{Contributions.}
The main contributions are:
\textbf{(1) RoPE-conditioned Q/K geometry}, a frequency-local functional metric derived
from the relative-position attention interaction;
\textbf{(2) matched optimizer evaluation}, separating the ORBIT update from recipe
selection across unseen seeds;
\textbf{(3) mechanism ablations}, isolating functional conditioning, RoPE-aware rotation,
and full frequency-pair coupling; and
\textbf{(4) transfer evaluation}, extending the protocol to a longer training horizon and
a 355M model with measured runtime and memory cost.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Matrix and spectral optimization.}
Muon replaces a raw matrix-gradient step with a momentum-smoothed, approximately
orthogonalized direction~\cite{jordan2024muon}; later work showed that weight decay and
update-scale calibration make this family viable at substantially larger language-model
scale~\cite{liu2025muon}. More general structured preconditioners include K-FAC, which
approximates layerwise Fisher blocks with Kronecker factors~\cite{martens2015kfac}, and
Shampoo, which maintains dimension-wise preconditioners from gradient statistics
~\cite{gupta2018shampoo}. PRISM adds anisotropic spectral shaping to Muon-like first-order
updates using low-rank curvature information~\cite{yang2026prism}. ORBIT differs from
these approaches by deriving its small Q/K metric from the known RoPE-conditioned
attention interaction and opposite-side activation statistics.

\paragraph{Optimization under architectural geometry.}
Recent work increasingly argues that optimizers should respect invariances or induced
weight-space geometry. \emph{Riemannian Gradient Descent for Low-Rank
Architectures}~\cite{knight2026} treats QK/VO products as low-rank optimization objects.
\emph{Dead-Direction Conditioners}~\cite{shirodkar2026ddc} develops gauge-equivariant
conditioners for architectural symmetries, including a per-head attention rotation matched
to RoPE. \emph{The Loss Does Not See the Basis, but Adam Does}~\cite{singh2026lossbasis}
shows that optimizer behavior can depend on arbitrary factor bases even when the represented
function is unchanged, with Q/K products serving as attention invariants. In
parameter-efficient adaptation, LoRA-TSD and ISO-LoRA optimize the induced tangent-space
weight update rather than treating low-rank factors independently
~\cite{andriianov2026loratsd,zhu2026isolora}. ORBIT is complementary: it constructs a local
training metric directly from the RoPE-conditioned Q/K bilinear function.

\paragraph{Query--key and RoPE structure.}
\emph{Faster Query-Key Learning Sharpens Attention}~\cite{vashisht2026fasterqk} shows
that the relative learning speed of QK and OV circuits changes attention concentration,
reinforcing the distinct functional role of QK optimization. \emph{Spectral Query-Key
Product Weight Steering}~\cite{tiwari2026qksteering} directly edits singular modes of the
query--key product as a training-free intervention. RoFormer introduced the rotation
structure that turns absolute Q/K rotations into relative-position interactions
~\cite{su2021roformer}; LeRoPE makes those frequencies learnable
~\cite{karypis2026lerope}; and \emph{RoPE Attention Is an Exact Forward-Pass Gradient Step
with Softmax Intact}~\cite{huang2026ropegradient} derives an exact gradient-step
representation of the RoPE-softmax forward pass. These works analyze or modify attention
geometry, but do not use the relative-position rotation itself to precondition Q/K
parameter updates during pretraining.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
To our knowledge, prior work has not introduced a training optimizer whose Q/K
preconditioner is derived directly from the RoPE-conditioned relative-position bilinear
interaction and implemented with per-frequency $2\times2$ statistics. ORBIT's contribution
is this training-time metric and the controlled experiments used to isolate its effect,
rather than spectral descent, QK-product optimization, architecture-aware optimization,
RoPE geometry, or gauge-equivariant conditioning.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Matched shared hyperparameters isolate the update-rule difference}
The primary comparison controls recipe differences by evaluating Muon and ORBIT on the
same ten deterministic 900-step candidate configurations on one tuning seed. Both methods
select the same winner (\MatchedSharedConfigID{}), which is frozen before the held-out
paired evaluation. Under that shared configuration, Muon reached mean validation loss
\MatchedMuonLoss{} and ORBIT reached \MatchedOrbitLoss{} across ten new paired seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:matched}).

The paired ORBIT-minus-Muon difference was \MatchedDelta{} nats, with a 95\% confidence
interval of [\MatchedCILow{}, \MatchedCIHigh{}].
ORBIT has the lower validation loss on \MatchedWins{} of the ten held-out paired runs
(Table~\ref{tab:matched}). Because the held-out comparison uses an identical frozen
configuration for both methods, this contrast estimates their update-rule difference at
that shared recipe; uncertainty from the preceding recipe-selection step is not included
in the confidence interval.

The mean wall-clock-time difference is \MatchedRuntimeOverhead{} relative to Muon, and
the recorded peak CUDA-allocation difference is \MatchedMemoryOverhead{}.

\subsection{Crossed recipes separate update-rule and recipe effects}
The optimizer-by-configuration cross-over makes the tuning confound explicit. When both
methods use the Muon-selected recipe, the ORBIT-minus-Muon difference is
\XConfigMechanismMuon{} nats, with ORBIT lower on
\XConfigMechanismMuonWins{} paired seeds. Under the ORBIT-selected recipe, the corresponding
difference is \XConfigMechanismOrbit{} nats, with ORBIT lower on
\XConfigMechanismOrbitWins{} paired seeds. These cells quantify how the update-rule
difference changes with the surrounding hyperparameters.

Recipe choice itself has a larger effect than the matched mechanism contrast. Moving Muon
from its own discovery recipe to the ORBIT-selected recipe changes validation loss by
\XConfigRecipeMuon{} nats, while the analogous change for ORBIT is
\XConfigRecipeOrbit{} nats. The cross-over separates update-rule and recipe effects without treating the independently
tuned gap as a mechanism-only estimate.

\subsection{Ablations isolate functional Q/K conditioning and RoPE-aware transport}
The expanded ten-seed ablation tests each component under the same matched recipe and
ORBIT statistics path. Relative to the \textbf{identity control}, the full-minus-control
difference is \AblIdentityDelta{} nats, with 95\% CI
[\AblIdentityCILow{}, \AblIdentityCIHigh{}], and full ORBIT is lower on
\AblIdentityWins{} paired seeds.

Relative to the \textbf{no-RoPE control}, which retains opposite-side second-moment
conditioning but removes relative-position transport, the difference is
\AblNoRoPEDelta{} nats, with 95\% CI
[\AblNoRoPECILow{}, \AblNoRoPECIHigh{}], and full ORBIT is lower on
\AblNoRoPEWins{} paired seeds.

Relative to the \textbf{diagonal control}, which removes off-diagonal coupling within each
transported $2\times2$ metric, the difference is \AblDiagDelta{} nats, with 95\% CI
[\AblDiagCILow{}, \AblDiagCIHigh{}], and full ORBIT is lower on
\AblDiagWins{} paired seeds. Together these contrasts measure the incremental contribution
of the three design choices without assuming their direction in advance
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}).

\subsection{Transfer checks across training horizon and model size}
The transfer experiments preserve each optimizer's independently frozen recipe and probe

\section{Discussion}
The matched experiment is the primary estimate because it holds the selected training
recipe fixed between Muon and ORBIT. Its paired mean, confidence interval, and win count
provide the direct evidence for the sign and magnitude of the update-rule difference.

The three ablations answer separate mechanism questions: identity tests whether the
function-space preconditioner matters at all; no-RoPE tests the incremental effect of causal
relative-position transport; and diagonal tests the incremental effect of off-diagonal
coupling within each frequency pair. Their paired intervals should be interpreted
individually rather than combined into a single aggregate effect.

The longer-horizon and 355M cells are deliberately secondary: each uses only two seeds and
independently frozen recipes, while the 355M cell also changes batch size and uses gradient
checkpointing. Their effect sizes are therefore not used to strengthen or rescale the
matched 900-step estimate.

\paragraph{Scope and future work.}
The present study evaluates RoPE-modified GPT-2-style decoders with multi-head attention.
The transfer experiments use two seeds, and the 355M setting uses a smaller batch size than
the 124M setting. Extending the evaluation to grouped-query attention, QK normalization,
mixture-of-experts models, larger pretraining budgets, lower-overhead implementations, and
controlled scaling studies on substantially larger-parameter models is a direct direction
for future work. More generally, ORBIT suggests a research program in which optimizer
geometry is specialized to architectural computations whenever an inexpensive local
functional metric can be derived. Independent replication and extension across broader
architectures and compute regimes are encouraged, using the accompanying implementation as
a basis for further investigation of function-space conditioning.

\section{Conclusion}
ORBIT introduces a training-time function-space preconditioner for rotary query--key
interactions. By constructing frequency-local metrics from Q/K statistics transported
through causal RoPE relative-position rotations, ORBIT adapts Muon's matrix update to the
computation performed by attention.

Under the same frozen shared training recipe, the primary experiment measures the paired
ORBIT--Muon effect on ten held-out seeds. Crossed-recipe and ablation experiments separate
recipe sensitivity from the contributions of functional Q/K conditioning, RoPE-aware
transport, and full frequency-pair coupling; additional experiments probe longer-horizon
and 355M transfer.

The study evaluates \emph{function-aware optimization} as a broader design direction:
optimizer geometry may be derived from the computation performed by a parameter block, not
only from tensor shape or generic gradient statistics. ORBIT provides one concrete
instantiation of this principle for rotary Q/K interactions, with the matched comparison
and mechanism ablations providing the primary empirical support.
