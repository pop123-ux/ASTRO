\begin{abstract}
Matrix optimizers such as Muon exploit the spectral structure of parameter matrices while
treating query and key projections as ordinary matrix parameters. ORBIT introduces a
RoPE-informed extension of Muon for rotary attention: frequency-local $2\times2$ metrics
are constructed from query/key second moments transported through optimizer-side rotary
transforms, applied to the candidate Q/K updates, and followed by joint Frobenius-norm
restoration. For a causal separation $\Delta=i-j\ge0$, the forward RoPE score contains
$R(-\Delta)$, whereas the optimizer evaluated here deliberately transports statistics with
the inverse orientation $R(+\Delta)$; this is a designed optimization geometry rather than
an exact score-side pullback. On RoPE-modified GPT-2-style decoders trained on FineWeb-Edu,
the evaluation separates optimizer mechanism from hyperparameter recipe. Muon and ORBIT are
evaluated on the same predeclared ten-candidate 900-step tuning grid; both independently
select the same configuration, which is then frozen before held-out paired evaluation.
Under that shared recipe, the comparison reports \MatchedWins{} ORBIT-favoring seeds and a
paired ORBIT-minus-Muon mean difference of \MatchedDelta{} nats (95\% CI
[\MatchedCILow{}, \MatchedCIHigh{}]). Mean end-to-end wall-clock difference is
\MatchedRuntimeOverhead{}, with peak allocated CUDA-memory difference
\MatchedMemoryOverhead{}. Cross-configuration experiments quantify recipe sensitivity,
while ablations isolate opposite-side Q/K conditioning, rotary transport, and full
frequency-pair coupling. A longer-horizon 124M experiment is reported as a descriptive
transfer check. More broadly, the study tests \emph{function-aware optimization} as a
design principle: optimizer geometry can be specialized to the computation performed by a
parameter block rather than determined only by tensor shape or gradient history.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Matrix optimizers have become a practical alternative to coordinate-wise adaptive methods
for Transformer training. Muon, for example, applies momentum followed by approximate
matrix orthogonalization to hidden two-dimensional parameters, and its scaled variants have
shown that spectral update geometry can remain effective at large language-model
scale~\cite{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon}. Yet this treatment is largely parameter-centric:
two matrices with the same shape receive the same class of update even when they participate
in qualitatively different computations.

Self-attention provides a particularly sharp example. Query and key projections jointly
determine pre-softmax attention scores, and RoPE rotates two-dimensional Q/K coordinate
pairs so that their interaction depends on relative position~\cite{su2021roformer}. For a
frequency pair $f$ and causal displacement $\Delta=i-j\ge0$, the forward computation gives
$q_{i,f}^{\top}R_f(-\Delta)k_{j,f}$. This exposes a distinguished two-dimensional rotary
coordinate system in which Q/K interactions vary with relative position.

ORBIT uses that known structure to define an optimizer-side geometry on top of Muon's
spectral update. It first forms the ordinary Muon-style candidate Q/K directions, then
preconditions each RoPE frequency pair with a compact metric built from the uncentered
second moment of the opposite circuit after transport over a fixed log-spaced distance
grid. The evaluated optimizer uses $R_f(+\Delta)$ for this transport---the inverse
orientation of the forward score's $R_f(-\Delta)$. This is an explicit optimizer design
choice, not a claim that ORBIT implements the exact score-gradient metric. A single joint
Frobenius restoration prevents the conditioning from changing the total Q/K step budget.
The distinctive ORBIT operations are local $2\times2$ matrix functions. This provides a
concrete instance of \emph{function-aware optimization}: the optimizer uses the known
structure of the computation in which Q/K parameters participate to define a local update
geometry, rather than relying only on generic parameter shape or gradient statistics.

Optimizer comparisons are unusually sensitive to tuning policy. For the primary comparison,
Muon and ORBIT are evaluated on the same ten deterministic candidates on one tuning seed.
Each method independently selects its lowest-loss candidate; in the reported campaign the
two winners coincide, yielding one shared recipe that is frozen before held-out paired
evaluation. Separate crossed-recipe experiments quantify optimizer--recipe interaction.
Full selection and evaluation details are given in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-protocol}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
The main contributions are:
\textbf{(1) RoPE-informed Q/K optimizer geometry}, a frequency-local preconditioner built
from opposite-side second moments and explicit rotary transport;
\textbf{(2) matched optimizer evaluation}, separating the ORBIT update from recipe
selection across unseen seeds while reporting paired effect size, runtime, and memory cost;
\textbf{(3) mechanism ablations}, isolating opposite-side conditioning, rotary transport,
and full frequency-pair coupling; and
\textbf{(4) transfer evaluation}, extending the comparison to a longer 124M training
horizon as a descriptive check outside the primary setting.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Matrix and spectral optimization.}
Muon replaces a raw matrix-gradient step with a momentum-smoothed, approximately
orthogonalized direction~\cite{jordan2024muon}; later work showed that weight decay and
update-scale calibration make this family viable at substantially larger language-model
scale~\cite{liu2025muon}. More general structured preconditioners include K-FAC, which
approximates layerwise Fisher blocks with Kronecker factors~\cite{martens2015kfac}, and
Shampoo, which maintains dimension-wise preconditioners from gradient statistics
~\cite{gupta2018shampoo}. PRISM adds anisotropic spectral shaping to Muon-like first-order
updates using low-rank curvature information~\cite{yang2026prism}. ORBIT differs from
these approaches by constructing a compact Q/K metric from RoPE frequency structure and
opposite-side activation statistics.

\paragraph{Optimization under architectural geometry.}
Recent work increasingly argues that optimizers should respect invariances or induced
weight-space geometry. \emph{Riemannian Gradient Descent for Low-Rank
Architectures}~\cite{knight2026} treats QK/VO products as low-rank optimization objects.
\emph{Dead-Direction Conditioners}~\cite{shirodkar2026ddc} develops gauge-equivariant
conditioners for architectural symmetries, including a per-head attention rotation matched
to RoPE. \emph{The Loss Does Not See the Basis, but Adam Does}~\cite{singh2026lossbasis}
shows that optimizer behavior can depend on arbitrary factor bases even when the represented
function is unchanged, with Q/K products serving as attention invariants. In
parameter-efficient adaptation, LoRA-TSD and ISO-LoRA optimize the induced tangent-space
weight update rather than treating low-rank factors independently
~\cite{andriianov2026loratsd,zhu2026isolora}. ORBIT is complementary: it uses architectural
Q/K and RoPE structure to define a small training-time preconditioner without constructing
a full function-space pullback.

\paragraph{Query--key and RoPE structure.}
\emph{Faster Query-Key Learning Sharpens Attention}~\cite{vashisht2026fasterqk} shows
that the relative learning speed of QK and OV circuits changes attention concentration,
reinforcing the distinct functional role of QK optimization. \emph{Spectral Query-Key
Product Weight Steering}~\cite{tiwari2026qksteering} directly edits singular modes of the
query--key product as a training-free intervention. RoFormer introduced the rotation
structure that turns absolute Q/K rotations into relative-position interactions
~\cite{su2021roformer}; LeRoPE makes those frequencies learnable
~\cite{karypis2026lerope}; and \emph{RoPE Attention Is an Exact Forward-Pass Gradient Step
with Softmax Intact}~\cite{huang2026ropegradient} derives an exact gradient-step
representation of the RoPE-softmax forward pass. These works analyze or modify attention
geometry, but do not use opposite-side Q/K second moments together with optimizer-side
rotary transport to precondition Q/K parameter updates during pretraining.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
The cited prior work does not introduce the specific training-time construction evaluated
here: per-frequency $2\times2$ Q/K metrics built from opposite-side activation statistics,
transported with the optimizer orientation $R(+\Delta)$, applied to Muon candidates, and
followed by one joint Q/K Frobenius restoration. ORBIT's contribution is this optimizer
geometry and the controlled experiments used to isolate its effect, rather than a claim that
the metric is an exact causal-score pullback.

\subsection{Shared hyperparameters isolate the update-rule difference}
The primary comparison evaluates Muon and ORBIT on the same ten deterministic 900-step
candidate configurations on one tuning seed. Each method independently selects its
lowest-loss candidate, and the two winners coincide. That common configuration is frozen
before held-out evaluation. Under the shared configuration, Muon reached mean validation
loss \MatchedMuonLoss{} and ORBIT reached \MatchedOrbitLoss{} across ten held-out paired
seeds (Table~\ref{tab:matched}).

The paired ORBIT-minus-Muon difference was \MatchedDelta{} nats, with a 95\% confidence
interval of [\MatchedCILow{}, \MatchedCIHigh{}]. ORBIT has the lower validation loss on
\MatchedWins{} of the ten held-out paired runs (Table~\ref{tab:matched}). Because the
held-out comparison uses an identical frozen configuration for both methods, this contrast
estimates their update-rule difference at that shared recipe; uncertainty from the preceding
recipe-selection step is not included in the confidence interval.

The mean end-to-end wall-clock difference is \MatchedRuntimeOverhead{} relative to Muon,

\section{Discussion}
The matched experiment is the primary estimate because it holds the selected training
recipe fixed between Muon and ORBIT. Its paired mean, confidence interval, and win count
provide the direct evidence for the sign and magnitude of the update-rule difference.

The three ablations answer separate mechanism questions: identity tests whether the
function-aware preconditioner matters at all; no-RoPE tests the incremental effect of rotary
transport; and diagonal tests the incremental effect of off-diagonal coupling within each
frequency pair. Their paired intervals should be interpreted individually rather than
combined into a single aggregate effect.

The longer-horizon 124M cell is deliberately secondary: it uses only two seeds and
independently frozen recipes, so its effect sizes are not used to strengthen or rescale the
matched 900-step estimate.

\paragraph{Scope and future work.}
The release analysis centers on non-checkpointed 124M RoPE-modified GPT-2-style decoders
with multi-head attention. An exploratory 355M checkpointed development cell is excluded
from the release analysis because the historical checkpoint path updated ORBIT statistics
under different recomputation semantics from the reusable package. Extending the evaluation
to grouped-query attention, QK normalization, mixture-of-experts models, larger pretraining
budgets, lower-overhead implementations, and controlled scaling studies on larger models is
a direct direction for future work. More generally, ORBIT suggests a research program in
which optimizer geometry is specialized to architectural computations whenever an
inexpensive local metric can be constructed. Independent replication and extension across
broader architectures and compute regimes are encouraged, using the accompanying
implementation as a basis for further investigation of function-aware conditioning.

\section{Conclusion}
ORBIT introduces a training-time function-aware preconditioner for rotary query--key
interactions. It constructs frequency-local metrics from opposite-side Q/K statistics and
an explicit optimizer-side $R(+\Delta)$ rotary transport, reshaping Muon's Q/K candidate
directions before restoring their joint Frobenius step budget. The forward attention score
itself contains $R(-\Delta)$; ORBIT's inverse orientation is therefore an optimizer design
choice rather than an exact score-side pullback.

Under the same frozen shared training recipe, the primary experiment measures the paired
ORBIT--Muon effect on ten held-out seeds. Crossed-recipe and ablation experiments separate
recipe sensitivity from the contributions of opposite-side Q/K conditioning, rotary
transport, and full frequency-pair coupling; an additional experiment probes longer-horizon
124M transfer.

The study evaluates \emph{function-aware optimization} as a broader design direction:
optimizer geometry may be informed by the computation performed by a parameter block, not
only by tensor shape or generic gradient statistics. ORBIT provides one concrete
instantiation of this principle for rotary Q/K interactions, with the matched comparison
and mechanism ablations providing the primary empirical support.

More broadly, function-aware optimization suggests moving beyond a single update geometry
applied uniformly across an entire network. Different parameter groups implement different
computations---rotary query--key projections, normalization, embeddings, routing, and other
specialized mechanisms---and can therefore warrant different training geometries. ORBIT
explores one such specialization for RoPE. A longer-term direction is to compose multiple
function-specific update rules within the same model, so that each parameter block is
optimized according to the computation it performs, with the aim of reducing loss and
improving training efficiency. Instead of one optimizer geometry for an entire model,
future training may use the right geometry for each computation.
