\begin{abstract}
Matrix optimizers such as Muon exploit the spectral structure of parameter matrices but do
not distinguish the function induced by rotary query--key interactions. We introduce ORBIT,
an optimizer-only extension of Muon that constructs frequency-local $2\times2$ metrics from
query/key covariances transported through RoPE relative-position rotations, preconditions
the candidate Q/K updates, and restores their joint Frobenius norm. On RoPE-modified
GPT-2-style decoders trained on FineWeb-Edu, we explicitly separate optimizer mechanism
from hyperparameter recipe. Under an identical configuration selected independently by both
methods from the same ten-candidate grid, ORBIT lowers validation loss on
\MatchedWins{} held-out seeds, with paired mean difference \MatchedDelta{} nats
(95\% CI [\MatchedCILow{}, \MatchedCIHigh{}]), at \MatchedRuntimeOverhead{} mean
runtime overhead and no material increase in peak allocated memory. Cross-configuration
experiments show that recipe choice explains a substantial fraction of the much larger
independently tuned gap. Expanded ablations support both functional Q/K conditioning and a
RoPE-specific contribution, while evidence for the full off-diagonal metric is weak.
In two-seed secondary transfer tests, the frozen ORBIT recipe remains lower-loss than the
frozen Muon and NorMuon recipes at 2700 steps and at 355M parameters; the 355M comparison
with ASTRO is unresolved. ORBIT changes no inference-time computation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Matrix optimizers have become a practical alternative to coordinate-wise adaptive methods
for Transformer training. Muon, for example, applies momentum followed by approximate
matrix orthogonalization to hidden two-dimensional parameters, and its scaled variants have
shown that spectral update geometry can remain effective at large language-model
scale~\cite{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon}. Yet this treatment is largely parameter-centric:
two matrices with the same shape receive the same class of update even when they participate
in qualitatively different computations.

Self-attention provides a particularly sharp example. Query and key projections jointly
determine pre-softmax attention scores, and RoPE rotates two-dimensional Q/K coordinate
pairs so that their interaction depends on relative position~\cite{su2021roformer}. For a
frequency pair $f$, the relevant score contribution is
$q_{i,f}^{\top}R_f(i-j)k_{j,f}$. Consequently, an equal-sized perturbation of $W_Q$ or
$W_K$ can induce very different functional movement depending on the opposite circuit's
statistics and on the RoPE rotation associated with token displacement. Standard Muon does
not model this interaction.

ORBIT asks whether that missing functional geometry can be incorporated without replacing
Muon's efficient spectral update or changing inference. It first forms the ordinary
Muon-style candidate Q/K directions, then preconditions each RoPE frequency pair with a
small metric derived from the covariance of the opposite circuit after transport through a
fixed distribution of relative-position rotations. A single joint Frobenius restoration
prevents this conditioning from winning merely by increasing the total Q/K step norm. The
result is an optimizer-only modification whose unique operations are local $2\times2$
matrix functions.

Optimizer comparisons are unusually sensitive to tuning policy, so the empirical design is
part of the contribution. Rather than treating the largest independently tuned gap as the
effect of ORBIT, we first cross the Muon and ORBIT recipes, then make both methods tune over
the same candidate set and evaluate their independently selected winner on unseen paired
seeds. Only after that mechanism-isolated comparison do we interpret ablations and
longer-horizon/model-scale transfer.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
This work makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We derive a RoPE-conditioned local metric for Q/K optimization directly from the
  relative-position bilinear attention interaction and implement it using frequency-local
  $2\times2$ statistics with no inference-time modification.
  \item We isolate the optimizer mechanism from hyperparameter recipe using a
  cross-configuration experiment and a ten-seed matched-hyperparameter confirmation.
  \item We test the mechanism with identity, no-RoPE, and diagonal controls, finding clear
  support for functional Q/K conditioning and a RoPE-specific contribution but only weak
  evidence that full off-diagonal coupling is necessary.
  \item We report longer-horizon and 355M transfer alongside runtime and memory costs, while
  explicitly preserving unresolved and negative results.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Novelty Boundary}
\paragraph{Matrix and spectral optimization.}
Muon replaces a raw matrix-gradient step with a momentum-smoothed, approximately
orthogonalized direction~\cite{jordan2024muon}; later work showed that weight decay and
update-scale calibration make this family viable at substantially larger language-model
scale~\cite{liu2025muon}. PRISM adds anisotropic spectral shaping to Muon-like first-order
updates using low-rank curvature information~\cite{yang2026prism}. These methods motivate
ORBIT's spectral base update, but they do not specialize the metric to the functional role
of rotary Q/K interactions.

\paragraph{Optimization under architectural geometry.}
Recent work increasingly argues that optimizers should respect invariances or induced
weight-space geometry. Knight studies Riemannian optimization of rank-factored attention
parameters and explicitly treats QK/VO products as low-rank optimization
objects~\cite{knight2026}. DDC constructs gauge-equivariant conditioners for architectural
symmetries, including a per-head attention rotation matched to RoPE, and can compose with a
Muon base~\cite{shirodkar2026ddc}. Singh similarly shows that optimizer behavior can depend
on arbitrary factor bases even when the loss does not, with Q/K products serving as
attention invariants~\cite{singh2026lossbasis}. In parameter-efficient adaptation,
LoRA-TSD and ISO-LoRA optimize the induced tangent-space weight update rather than treating
low-rank factors independently~\cite{andriianov2026loratsd,zhu2026isolora}. ORBIT is
complementary: it does not optimize a low-rank factor manifold or quotient symmetry, but
constructs a local training metric from the actual RoPE-conditioned Q/K bilinear function.

\paragraph{Query--key and RoPE structure.}
Vashisht and Ramaswamy show that the relative learning speed of QK and OV circuits changes
attention concentration~\cite{vashisht2026fasterqk}, reinforcing that QK optimization has
a distinct functional role. QK Product Steering directly edits singular modes of the
query--key product, but as a training-free post-hoc intervention for VLM
hallucination~\cite{tiwari2026qksteering}. On the positional side, RoFormer introduced the
rotation structure that converts absolute Q/K rotations into relative-position
interactions~\cite{su2021roformer}; LeRoPE makes the rotation frequencies themselves
learnable~\cite{karypis2026lerope}; and Huang et al. derive an exact gradient-step
representation of the RoPE-softmax forward pass~\cite{huang2026ropegradient}. These works
analyze or modify the attention function, but do not use its relative-position rotation to
precondition Q/K parameter updates during pretraining.

\paragraph{Novelty boundary.}
To our knowledge, prior work has not introduced a training optimizer whose Q/K
preconditioner is derived from the RoPE-conditioned relative-position bilinear interaction
and implemented with per-frequency $2\times2$ statistics. We therefore make the narrow
claim that ORBIT contributes this specific training-time metric and its mechanism-isolated
evaluation. We do not claim to originate spectral descent, QK-product
optimization, architecture-aware optimization, RoPE geometry, or gauge-equivariant
conditioning.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Matched hyperparameters isolate a small but reproducible ORBIT effect}
The primary comparison removes optimizer-specific recipe selection as a confound. Muon and
ORBIT were presented with the same ten deterministic 900-step candidate configurations and
independently selected the same frozen candidate before any held-out confirmation seed was
evaluated. Under that shared configuration, Muon reached mean validation loss
\MatchedMuonLoss{} and ORBIT reached \MatchedOrbitLoss{} across ten new paired seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:matched}).

The paired ORBIT-minus-Muon difference was \MatchedDelta{} nats, with a 95\% confidence
interval of [\MatchedCILow{}, \MatchedCIHigh{}]. ORBIT obtained the lower validation loss
on \MatchedWins{} seeds (Figure~\ref{fig:matched}). This effect is much smaller than the
difference observed in the independently tuned broad campaign, but it is the quantity that
most directly estimates the optimizer mechanism under matched hyperparameters. The gain
therefore survives removal of independent-search luck rather than depending only on a more
favorable ORBIT recipe.

The improvement is not free computationally. Mean wall-clock time increased by
\MatchedRuntimeOverhead{} relative to Muon, while recorded peak CUDA allocation changed by
only \MatchedMemoryOverhead{}. In this regime ORBIT therefore trades a modest training-time
overhead for a small but repeatable loss improvement, without a material increase in peak
allocated GPU memory.

\subsection{Hyperparameter recipe accounts for a substantial part of the broad gap}
The optimizer-by-configuration cross-over makes the tuning confound explicit. When both
methods use the Muon-selected recipe, ORBIT improves on Muon by
\XConfigMechanismMuon{} nats and wins \XConfigMechanismMuonWins{} paired seeds. When both
use the ORBIT-selected recipe, the corresponding mechanism difference is
\XConfigMechanismOrbit{} nats, again with ORBIT winning
\XConfigMechanismOrbitWins{} seeds. Thus the ORBIT update improves the objective under both
recipes, but the size of the gain depends strongly on the surrounding hyperparameters.

Recipe choice itself has a larger effect than the matched mechanism contrast. Moving Muon
from its own discovery recipe to the ORBIT-selected recipe changes validation loss by
\XConfigRecipeMuon{} nats, while the analogous change for ORBIT is
\XConfigRecipeOrbit{} nats. The cross-over therefore rules out interpreting the original
independently tuned ORBIT--Muon difference as a pure algorithmic effect. Instead, ORBIT and
the surrounding training recipe interact: the mechanism is reproducible, while recipe
selection contributes substantially to the larger broad-campaign gap.

\subsection{Ablations support functional Q/K conditioning and a RoPE-specific contribution}
The expanded ten-seed ablation tests which pieces of ORBIT are responsible for the matched
effect. The identity control removes the functional preconditioner while preserving the
same matched recipe and ORBIT update path. Full ORBIT improves on this control by
\AblIdentityDelta{} nats, with 95\% CI
[\AblIdentityCILow{}, \AblIdentityCIHigh{}], and wins \AblIdentityWins{} seeds. The
magnitude is close to the independent matched Muon comparison, providing a second
mechanism-focused estimate on a different seed set.

Removing the RoPE rotations while retaining generic Q/K functional adaptation weakens the
optimizer further: full ORBIT improves on the no-RoPE control by
\AblNoRoPEDelta{} nats, with 95\% CI
[\AblNoRoPECILow{}, \AblNoRoPECIHigh{}], and wins \AblNoRoPEWins{} seeds. This comparison
supports the claim that relative-position-aware conditioning contributes beyond generic
Q/K covariance adaptation in the tested architecture.

The diagonal control is a more delicate result. Full ORBIT improves on it by only
\AblDiagDelta{} nats, with 95\% CI
[\AblDiagCILow{}, \AblDiagCIHigh{}], and wins \AblDiagWins{} seeds. The interval lies only
marginally below zero, and the effect is much smaller than the identity and no-RoPE
contrasts. We therefore report this as weak evidence for the full off-diagonal
frequency-pair metric, not as evidence that off-diagonal phase coupling is essential to
ORBIT's benefit.

\subsection{The frozen ORBIT recipe transfers across horizon and model size}
Unlike the primary matched confirmation, these transfer cells preserve each optimizer's
independently frozen recipe. They therefore test recipe-level persistence and transfer, not
the matched ORBIT mechanism estimand.

At 124M and 2700 steps, the frozen ORBIT recipe remains below each comparison recipe in both
tested seeds.
Its mean paired differences are \HorizonVsMuon{} nats versus Muon,
\HorizonVsNorMuon{} versus NorMuon, and \HorizonVsAstro{} versus ASTRO. The two
ORBIT--ASTRO seed differences are closely aligned in sign and magnitude. Because this stage contains only two paired seeds per optimizer, we treat the result as
secondary evidence that the frozen ORBIT recipe can remain effective over a substantially
longer training horizon, not as a high-powered inferential comparison or a matched
mechanism estimate.

At 355M and 900 steps, the frozen ORBIT recipe again beats the frozen Muon and NorMuon
recipes on both tested seeds, with mean
paired differences of \ScaleVsMuon{} and \ScaleVsNorMuon{} nats respectively. The ASTRO
comparison is unresolved: the mean ORBIT-minus-ASTRO difference is \ScaleVsAstro{} nats,
but one seed favors ORBIT and the other favors ASTRO. We therefore do not claim superiority
over ASTRO at 355M. Because the larger model also uses a smaller batch size and hence a
different sampled-token budget per optimizer step, these experiments demonstrate transfer
to a larger model but do not isolate model scale as the cause of any change in effect size.

\subsection{Independently tuned baselines provide context, not the causal estimate}
The broad 124M confirmation places ORBIT among the stronger recipes in the campaign.
ASTRO reaches mean validation loss \BroadAstroLoss{}, ORBIT \BroadOrbitLoss{}, NorMuon
\BroadNorMuonLoss{}, and Muon \BroadMuonLoss{}. ORBIT and ASTRO are therefore numerically close at this horizon under their independently
selected recipes, while both outperform the original Muon recipe by a much larger margin.

These numbers are useful for practical context, but they are not the primary estimate of
ORBIT's algorithmic effect because each optimizer enters the comparison with a separately
selected configuration. The matched confirmation and cross-over experiments above are what
separate optimizer mechanism from recipe selection.

\section{Discussion}
Three conclusions follow from the complete campaign. First, the clean mechanism effect is
reproducible in this setting but modest: after forcing Muon and ORBIT onto the same
hyperparameter configuration,
ORBIT still improves validation loss across unseen seeds, but by far less than the original
independently tuned comparison suggested. This distinction matters for optimizer research,
where a limited search budget can easily fold recipe quality into the apparent value of a
new update rule.

Second, the ablations localize most of the evidence to the functional Q/K preconditioner and
to its use of relative-position-aware RoPE geometry. The close agreement between the
matched Muon comparison and the identity-control ablation is particularly useful because
the two estimates use different seed sets and different experimental constructions. By
contrast, the off-diagonal part of the $2\times2$ metric contributes only a very small
increment in the present setting. A simpler diagonal approximation may therefore deserve
future study, but selecting it as the final method after observing these ablations would be
post-hoc and is deliberately avoided here.

Third, the frozen ORBIT recipe's lower validation loss is not confined to the original
900-step 124M setting. It remains lower than the frozen Muon and NorMuon recipes at 2700
steps and at 355M in the two tested seeds, and it is lower than ASTRO at the long horizon.
Because these transfer cells retain independently selected recipes, they do not isolate the
ORBIT mechanism. The 355M comparison with ASTRO remains unresolved. Together, these
observations support ORBIT as a lightweight geometry-aware modification to Muon-family
training while preserving a clear boundary between mechanism-isolated and recipe-level
evidence.

\section{Limitations}
The local metric is an approximation to rotary attention-logit function space rather than
an exact natural gradient. The matched effect is smaller than the original independently
tuned ORBIT--Muon gap, demonstrating that tuning recipe materially affects headline
optimizer comparisons. The off-diagonal ablation provides only weak evidence of an additional benefit beyond the
diagonal approximation. The long-horizon and 355M transfer cells use only two paired seeds and
therefore provide transfer evidence rather than high-powered inferential tests. The 355M
batch/token regime also differs from the 124M experiments, so the paper does not infer that
the ORBIT advantage grows with model scale from these cells alone. Finally, all experiments
use a RoPE-modified GPT-2-style decoder with multi-head attention; the present evidence does
not establish transfer to modern grouped-query attention, QK normalization, mixture-of-
experts architectures, or substantially larger pretraining budgets.

\section{Conclusion}
ORBIT tests a narrow hypothesis: when RoPE makes the query--key interaction explicitly
relative-position dependent, the optimizer can benefit from measuring Q/K updates in that
induced function space rather than only in raw parameter space. The answer in our controlled
campaign is positive but deliberately modest. At identical hyperparameters selected from an
identical search grid, ORBIT improves over Muon on \MatchedWins{} unseen seeds with a mean
paired difference of \MatchedDelta{} nats, while incurring \MatchedRuntimeOverhead{} mean
runtime overhead and essentially unchanged peak allocated memory. Cross-configuration
experiments show that hyperparameter recipe explains a substantial part of the larger
independently tuned gap, underscoring why that gap should not be attributed to the update
rule alone.

Mechanism ablations support functional Q/K conditioning and a specifically RoPE-aware
component, but provide only weak evidence for the full off-diagonal $2\times2$ coupling.
Secondary longer-horizon and 355M experiments suggest that the frozen ORBIT recipe can
transfer beyond the primary setting, while the 355M comparison with ASTRO remains
unresolved. The resulting picture is not that ORBIT
universally dominates strong optimizer recipes, but that a small, reproducible gain remains
after tuning effects are removed. More broadly, the results support optimizer design that
targets the computation induced by a parameter block, while also showing the importance of
matched tuning and explicit negative controls when evaluating such methods.

\section{Reproducibility}
All runs are append-only JSONL records containing the optimizer configuration, random seed,
code digest, Python/package environment, GPU model, wall-clock time, validation loss,
training curve, and peak CUDA allocation. Paper generation is gated by a strict evidence
freeze that checks exact task counts, expected seeds, the frozen core digest, a common
software environment, identical matched configurations, and finite metrics before writing a
manifest, claim ledger, tables, or figures.
